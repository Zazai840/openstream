# One-model dictation candidates

Research note for [issue #179](https://github.com/Nabzx/openstream/issues/179), "Which open local speech models fit the one-model dictation budget?"

- Researched: 2026-08-27.
- Scope: open local speech models, first-party model cards and repositories, official runtime/export documentation, and the published model licences.
- Interpretation: "one model" means one speech model, not an ASR model followed by a second generative rewrite model. A model that only transcribes speech is not credited with arbitrary text cleanup.

## Recommendation

No candidate satisfies every constraint in the ticket as documented. The hard conflict is cleanup. The model cards describe ASR, speech-to-text, or speech translation. None documents a general dictation rewrite operation that safely removes every stutter, filler, false start, repetition, or spoken correction. Punctuation and capitalization are documented by several models, but those are not the same task.

For a first local benchmark, use **NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming 0.6B**. NVIDIA publishes a 742 MB Q8 GGUF, an official `NeMo-Speech.cpp` runtime that selects Metal on Apple Silicon, cache-aware streaming with 80 ms through 1.12 s chunks, 40 language-locales, and built-in punctuation and capitalization. Those facts make it the best documented fit for the latency, size, multilingual ASR, and formatting parts of the ticket. It does not provide speech translation, and NVIDIA's published latency results are on an H100, not a Mac, so the one-second cursor budget remains unproven. Its OpenMDW-1.1 licence also needs to be accepted and redistributed with the model.

If translation from supported languages into English is mandatory in the same model, benchmark **Whisper small in `whisper.cpp`** instead. Its official GGML artifact is 466 MiB, the model is multilingual and translates speech to English, and `whisper.cpp` supports Apple Silicon, Metal, and Core ML. The tradeoff is that OpenAI says Whisper is not real-time out of the box, and the official runtime does not document the requested end-of-speech latency on Apple Silicon. It also does not document dictation cleanup.

**Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3** is the strongest NVIDIA alternative when translation is not required and the target languages are its 25 European languages. Its official 714 MB Q8 GGUF clears the artifact limit. It is self-punctuating and has good published ASR scores. However, NVIDIA's official local runtime says v3 is full-utterance only and rejects streaming requests. It therefore needs a Mac end-to-end benchmark before it can be considered for the one-second budget.

## What the sources do and do not establish

### Artifact size and RAM

The strict size check must use the exact artifact shipped, not the parameter count. Canary's 978M parameters still produce a 6.36 GB `.nemo`; Parakeet v2's 600M parameters produce a 2.47 GB `.nemo`; and Parakeet v3's official Q8 export is 714 MB while its safetensors file is 2.51 GB. Quantization is therefore decisive.

For the likely candidates, the published figures are incomplete. NVIDIA states at least 2 GB RAM for Parakeet v2 and v3, and at least 6 GB for Canary. `whisper.cpp` reports about 852 MB runtime memory for small. Nemotron's model card does not give a Mac RSS figure. Moonshine publishes parameter counts and artifact sizes but the reviewed model guide does not give an OpenStream-style resident RSS figure. Measure peak RSS with the resident model, audio buffers, runtime, and application together.

### Local macOS execution

The official `NeMo-Speech.cpp` installer selects Metal on Apple Silicon and publishes local GGUF instructions. That establishes runtime availability for Nemotron 3.5 and Parakeet TDT v3. It does not establish their performance on a particular Mac. NeMo's original Python model cards list Linux and NVIDIA hardware, so using the C++ runtime is material.

`whisper.cpp` explicitly supports Mac Arm and Metal. WhisperKit is another official Apple-oriented runtime. Its README requires macOS 14 or later, supports Core ML models, offers multilingual Whisper variants, and exposes local transcription and translation endpoints. It documents a compressed `large-v3-v20240930_626MB` model, but the source reviewed here does not provide a target-Mac end-of-speech benchmark for it. The smaller `whisper.cpp` artifact has clearer size and memory figures, so it is the safer first Whisper comparison. [WhisperKit README](https://github.com/argmaxinc/argmax-oss-swift/blob/main/README.md)

### Latency

A chunk size is not the ticket's latency. The product budget starts at end of speech and ends when text appears at the cursor. It includes endpoint detection, final inference, any text cleanup, IPC, and macOS text injection. A resident model removes model-load time, but it does not remove those other costs.

Nemotron's 80 ms setting proves that the model has a low-latency streaming mode in its API, not that a Mac returns final text within one second. Parakeet TDT v3 is explicitly offline in the official runtime. Whisper is explicitly not real-time out of the box. Moonshine is the only reviewed source with a post-stop final-transcript metric and Mac measurements, but its qualifying streaming model is English-only.

The benchmark should record p50 and p95 for 1, 3, 5, and 10 second dictations on representative Apple Silicon machines. Keep each model resident. Measure endpoint-to-text, text-to-cursor, total end-to-cursor, peak RSS, and WER. Include short pauses, spoken punctuation, numbers, technical terms, and mixed-language samples. Do not treat H100 RTFx or a streaming chunk duration as a Mac cursor-latency result.

### Cleanup

All reviewed NVIDIA cards call these models ASR or speech-to-text models. Their documented formatting feature is punctuation and capitalization. The Canary and Parakeet cards say their training transcripts preserve punctuation and capitalization; they do not say that the decoder rewrites spontaneous speech. Nemotron says the same for its output. The model cards also warn that accuracy varies with speech type, context, noise, accent, and language.

OpenAI's official Whisper discussion records a maintainer explanation that Whisper may remove filler words and stutters because its training transcripts often omit them, while disfluencies remain in the vocabulary. That is not a controllable, evaluated cleanup feature. It can remove something that should remain, retain something that should be removed, or change a word through an ASR error.

Therefore the note gives no candidate credit for arbitrary cleanup. The safe conclusion is narrower: punctuation and capitalization can be model-provided where documented; filler/stutter/repetition removal and spoken self-correction require a targeted local benchmark, and reliable rewriting still needs an explicitly designed rewrite step or deterministic rules.

## Suggested benchmark decision

1. Benchmark **Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming 0.6B Q8** first for multilingual dictation latency, memory, punctuation, and accuracy.
2. Benchmark **Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 Q8** second as an accuracy comparison for its 25 European languages, using offline finalization.
3. Benchmark **Whisper small GGML** if translation to English is a hard requirement.
4. Keep **Moonshine streaming small** as the English latency baseline, not as the multilingual one-model answer.
5. Do not spend time on Canary-1B-v2 or the unquantized Parakeet v2/CTC artifacts unless the 1 GB limit changes.

The first benchmark should answer the unresolved questions rather than infer them from vendor hardware results:

- Does Nemotron 3.5 meet p95 end-of-speech-to-cursor under one second on each supported Mac tier?
- Does its 80/160/320 ms configuration trade accuracy for latency acceptably on short dictations?
- What are peak RSS and startup time for the resident Q8 GGUF inside OpenStream?
- How do Nemotron, Parakeet v3, Whisper small, and Moonshine handle filler words, repeated words, false starts, spoken corrections, numbers, punctuation commands, and technical vocabulary?
- Does any model's apparent disfluency removal hurt verbatim fidelity or delete intentional words?
- If translation is required, is Whisper small's quality and latency acceptable, or does the product need a second translation/rewrite role?
- What licence notices and model files must ship with the selected runtime and artifact?
